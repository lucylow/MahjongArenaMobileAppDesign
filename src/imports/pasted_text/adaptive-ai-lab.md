 Prompt 5 — “Adaptive AI Lab”: Practice Against Intelligent Opponent Personalities

```text
Design a mobile app called “Adaptive AI Lab,” a Mahjong training and gameplay application where users practice against AI opponents that have distinct strategies, personalities, and adaptive behavior. This is a high-fidelity mobile UI concept for a React Native + Expo application on iPhone and Android.

Use a 390 x 844 frame, safe-area-aware mobile layouts, a persistent bottom navigation labeled Home, AI Lab, Match, Review, and Profile, and large touch-friendly controls. The visual identity should combine a modern research laboratory with an elegant Mahjong room: deep indigo, jade green, warm cream, copper, and electric mint accents used sparingly. Use abstract neural-network patterns only as subtle background decoration. Keep the Mahjong board tactile and understandable.

Create these connected screens:

1. AI Lab home. Show the headline “Train against the way you want to improve.” Display three large training modes: “Read Aggression,” “Practice Defense,” and “Improve Efficiency.” Include a progress panel showing recent performance against different AI styles. Add a primary button labeled “Create Training Match.”

2. Opponent personality library. Show a scrollable collection of AI opponents with illustrated portraits, strategy profiles, difficulty, and adaptation status. Include profiles such as:

   “The Chaser” — prioritizes speed and frequent attacks.

   “The Architect” — builds high-value hands patiently.

   “The Wall” — folds early and minimizes deal-in risk.

   “The Opportunist” — changes strategy based on score and round context.

   “The Human-Like Player” — makes understandable, moderate decisions rather than maximizing every numerical edge.

Each card should include a mini radar chart for Speed, Value, Defense, Risk, and Adaptability. Keep the charts readable and decorative rather than pretending to provide scientifically exact measurements.

3. Build-your-opponent screen. Let users configure an AI opponent using sliders and segmented controls for aggression, hand value preference, defense, adaptation speed, explanation frequency, and conversational tone. Include presets called Balanced, Aggressive, Defensive, Experimental, and Human-Like. Add a short dynamic description that changes when settings change, for example: “This opponent will attack early but may overvalue speed when behind.”

4. AI memory and adaptation screen. Show what the opponent has learned during the current training session. Use cards titled “Your common discard pattern,” “Situations that challenge you,” “Opponent adjustment,” and “Next counter-strategy.” Example text: “You fold effectively after riichi but take more risk when defending against open melds. The AI will increase open-hand pressure in the next match.” Clearly state that this is a training profile and not a permanent judgment of the player.

5. Live match against an adaptive AI. Display the standard four-player Mahjong table with the selected AI opponent highlighted by a small profile badge. When the AI makes an important decision, show an optional “Why did they do that?” button. If the player taps it, open a short explanation with the AI’s current objective, such as “The AI is prioritizing speed because it is behind by 4,000 points.” Do not reveal impossible private information.

6. Strategy experiment screen. Let users choose a scenario before beginning: early hand, late hand, defending against riichi, chasing a low-value win, deciding whether to call, or protecting a lead. Show the scenario objective, starting state, target skill, and estimated duration. Include a “Start experiment” button.

7. Compare AI styles screen. Show the same Mahjong decision evaluated by several AI policies: Aggressive, Balanced, Defensive, and Human-Like. Display the chosen discard, reason, estimated risk, and strategic objective for each. Use side-by-side cards that stack vertically on mobile. Include a callout: “Different policies can choose different reasonable moves.”

8. Training results screen. Show improvement by skill category, not only win rate. Include Attack Timing, Defense, Hand Efficiency, Score Awareness, and Reading Opponents. Display a “Next best challenge” card and buttons labeled “Rematch with adjustment,” “Try another style,” and “Review key decisions.”

9. AI ethics and transparency screen. Explain that the opponent is configurable, that difficulty does not need to rely on instant reactions, and that the app aims to challenge decision-making rather than overwhelm the player with artificial speed. Include controls for reaction delay, hint frequency, and visible explanations. Include an accessibility mode with reduced motion, high contrast, larger tiles, and simplified language.

Design requirements: make AI differences visually legible through strategy badges, not only color. Use natural-language descriptions alongside metrics. Do not make every opponent look villainous or robotic. Keep the interface focused on learning specific skills. Avoid fake claims about “genius” or “perfect” AI and avoid ranking users only by a single rating number.
```
